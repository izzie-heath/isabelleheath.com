the idea

A camp built from three things I love

I came home for the summer wanting to combine my programming experience, my enthusiasm for teaching curious young minds, and my love for all things STEM. Camp Coderpillar is what hatched.

It’s a weekly summer camp where beginners ages 7–12 learn to code alongside hands-on science and other creative activities, in small groups and a supportive, beginner-friendly environment.

The website has two audiences: kids, who should find it fun, and parents, who need to understand the camp and sign up.

the website

Designed, then coded by hand

Five pages: Home, Camps, About me, FAQ, and Sign up. I designed the experience and built every page, plus the registration form, in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The FAQ answers the questions parents ask most, from age range and laptop requirements to the sibling discount and absence policy.

Working with an illustrator

from sign-up to day one

Registration and parent communication

Running camp meant a lot of emails. I automated the repetitive ones with Mailchimp so parents always knew what happened next.

  1. 01

    Register

    Parents sign up on the site: choose a camp and a morning or afternoon session, rent a laptop if needed, and opt in or out of photos.

  2. 02

    Automatic info

    Registering sends camp details and payment options by email, automated through Mailchimp.

  3. 03

    Welcome

    Once payment came in, families got a welcome email with the session recap and what to bring each day.

  4. 04

    Daily updates

    During camp I emailed parents a daily recap, also through Mailchimp.
